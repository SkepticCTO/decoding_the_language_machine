# Episode 1: Shannon's N-grams: Precursor to LLMs

This directory contains the production assets and resources for the first episode of the series: **"The Stochastic Dawn."**

## Episode Overview
Episode 1 takes us back to 1948 and the work of **Claude Shannon**. It explores how Shannon's "Mathematical Theory of Communication" and his experiments with "Approximations to English" (N-grams) birthed the information age and the foundational concepts behind today’s Large Language Models (LLMs). The central intuition is that **structure does not require understanding**, and that coherence can emerge from simple statistical probabilities.

## Key Concepts Explored
* **Information as "Surprise":** Defining information as the resolution of uncertainty.
* **The "Book Method":** Shannon's manual algorithm for generating statistical text.
* **N-gram Approximations:** How words and sentences emerge from letter-by-letter and word-by-word statistics.
* **The Stochastic Parrot:** A historical grounding for the concept of form without meaning.
